Beyond Multivitamins: Why Comprehensive Nutrition Matters

Bridgecare Editorial Team·8 min read·Evidence: Established·Last reviewed: July 2026

Good nutrition is built on dietary patterns, not a single nutrient or supplement.

Key takeaways

  • Food remains the foundation of nutrition.
  • Vitamins and minerals perform different physiological roles.
  • Omega-3 fats are part of a broader healthy-eating pattern.
  • Supplements may help fill specific gaps but are not substitutes for balanced meals.

The whole-diet approach

A varied diet can provide protein, healthy fats, fibre, vitamins, minerals and protective plant compounds. Needs change with age, pregnancy, illness, medicines and dietary restrictions.

When supplements may be considered

Supplement use may be appropriate when intake is inadequate, requirements are increased or a clinician identifies a specific need. Product choice and dose should be based on reliable information.

Responsible use

Check labels, avoid unnecessary duplication and discuss supplements with a healthcare professional when taking medicines or managing a health condition.

Important note

This article is for general education and does not replace personalised advice, diagnosis or treatment from a qualified healthcare professional.
